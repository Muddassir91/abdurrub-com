# Openings master doc

The single source of truth for writing openings for Muddassir Abdur Rub. Claude reads this in full before suggesting any opening, and applies every section every time. The `/openings` skill in `.claude/skills/openings/SKILL.md` runs this process.

---

## 1. What counts as an opening

Anything whose only job is to earn the next second of attention:

| Type | Where it lives | What it has to do |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Landing pages, sales pages, blog posts, advertorials | Stop the scroll or the scan, promise or provoke |
| Subheadline | Under the headline | Make the promise concrete or add the twist |
| Email subject line | Inbox | Win the open against 40 other lines |
| Email preview text | Inbox, next to the subject | Add tension, never repeat the subject |
| Email first line | First sentence of the body | Pull the reader into a scene or an argument |
| Ad hook (static) | First line of primary text, headline on the image | Stop the thumb |
| Ad hook (video / UGC) | First 1 to 3 seconds, spoken and on screen | Earn second 4 |
| Drama story ad opening | First line of the story | Make them need to know what happened |
| VSL hook | First 10 seconds | Make leaving feel like a loss |
| Script first line | Any spoken content | Same as the VSL hook, shorter |
| Lead / teaser | Blog intro, LinkedIn post first line, Substack intro | Earn the "see more" click |

Whenever Muddassir asks for any of these, this doc applies.

---

## 2. The process, every time

1. **Pin the brief.** Offer, audience, awareness level (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware), the format from the table above, and the single emotion the opening should trigger.
2. **Pull live tabloid headlines.** Fetch the outlets in section 4. Grab the 20 to 30 strongest current headlines. If the web is unavailable, say so and use the fallback patterns.
3. **Extract structures.** For each headline: trigger, specificity device, tension device. Write them as fill-in-the-blank patterns.
4. **Generate in three lanes.** Every batch of openings includes all three:
   - **Settle lane.** Openings built from the Ben Settle patterns in section 3.
   - **Tabloid lane.** Openings built from the live headlines, with the source headline or pattern shown.
   - **Direct lane.** Straight promise, proof or curiosity openings for the format, so there is always a safe control.
5. **Label every option.** Mark each as (Settle), (Tabloid: source) or (Direct). Muddassir decides, so he needs to see where each came from.
6. **Fit the format.** Apply the format rules in section 5 before presenting.
7. **Check voice.** No em or en dashes, active voice, short sentences, none of the banned words. Never position Muddassir as a copywriter.

Default batch size: 10 to 15 options unless asked otherwise, spread across the three lanes.

## 3. Ben Settle openings

Ben Settle (Email Players) writes daily emails built on "infotainment": entertain first, teach second, sell every time. His openings are short, odd and personal. They never give the content away in the subject line, and the first line pulls the reader into a scene or an argument before any teaching starts. These patterns transfer to every format in section 1, not just email.

### Subject line and headline patterns

1. **The odd, specific, half-sentence.** A phrase that makes no sense until you read on. Shape: "the goat that ate my Tuesday".
2. **The pop-culture or villain hook.** A character, film, wrestler or news villain used as a doorway into the lesson. Shape: "What Darth Vader knew about retention".
3. **The confession.** Admitting a mistake or embarrassing moment. Shape: "I nearly lost this client last week".
4. **The quote lead.** A striking line someone said to him, in quotation marks. Shape: "'Your emails are too long'".
5. **The question that bothers them.** A direct question aimed at a fear they already have. Shape: "Why do your flows stop making money after week 2?".
6. **The lowercase, one-to-three-word teaser.** Looks like a note from a friend, not a campaign. Shape: "weird", "about yesterday", "bad news".
7. **The contrarian flip.** A position against the accepted advice. Shape: "Stop writing welcome emails".
8. **The reader mailbag.** Framing the piece as a reply to a reader. Shape: "A reader asks about discount codes".
9. **The number with a twist.** A number that is suspiciously specific or deliberately unimpressive. Shape: "The 3 word subject line that out-pulled everything".
10. **The time or place anchor.** Starting mid-scene with a time, place or mundane detail. Shape: "Sunday, 6:12am, Lisbon".

### First line and hook patterns

1. **In medias res.** Open in the middle of a scene. "So there I was, staring at a Shopify dashboard at midnight..."
2. **The mundane-to-lesson bridge.** Something trivial from daily life (a dog, a drive, a bad meal) that turns into the point.
3. **The reader quote.** "Got this email from a reader yesterday:" then the quote, then the response.
4. **The contrarian declaration.** One sentence that picks a fight with the industry.
5. **The story in progress.** "Quick story." or "True story:" followed by a short anecdote.
6. **The confession opener.** "I have a confession."
7. **The 'here is what happened' recap.** Reporting a conversation, a client result or a screwup like a diary entry.
8. **The pre-frame.** "This will annoy some people." or "Not for everyone, but..."
9. **The one-word paragraph.** A single word on its own line, then the story.
10. **The direct address.** Speaking to one reader about one problem they have right now.

### Settle rules of thumb

- Subject line and first line never repeat each other. Same for headline and subheadline, hook and second line.
- Never explain the opening in the next line. Make them keep going to find out.
- Entertain before you educate. The lesson arrives late and the pitch arrives last.
- One idea per piece. The opening sets up exactly one tension.
- Personality over polish. Odd beats clever.

## 4. The tabloid method

Muddassir Abdur Rub and Matthew Volkwyn brainstormed an easy, completely overlooked way to source winning ideas for openings. It has already cranked out multiple top spenders for 8 and 9 figure brands.

**Look at drama media outlets and tabloids.**

That industry has been around far longer than ecom and spent over a century perfecting the art of hooking and retaining attention with diabolical headlines and stories. Take those headlines and stories and turn them into openings. Originally used for drama story ads, the method applies to every format in section 1: a tabloid headline structure works as a subject line, a landing page headline, a static ad hook or a VSL first line.

Credit: Michael Kleynhans for sharing the example pics that accompanied the original post.

### Outlets to check (US and UK)

| Outlet | Section to check | URL |
|---|---|---|
| Take a Break | Real life | https://www.takeabreak.co.uk/real-life/ |
| That's Life! | Real life stories | https://www.thatslife.co.uk |
| Chat | Real life | https://www.chatmagazine.co.uk |
| The Sun | Fabulous, real life | https://www.thesun.co.uk/fabulous/real-life/ |
| Daily Mirror | Real life stories | https://www.mirror.co.uk/news/real-life-stories/ |
| Daily Mail | Femail | https://www.dailymail.co.uk/femail/index.html |
| New York Post | Relationships tag | https://nypost.com/tag/relationships/ |
| National Enquirer | Homepage | https://www.nationalenquirer.com |
| TMZ | Homepage | https://www.tmz.com |
| E! News | Homepage | https://www.eonline.com |

### How to mine them

1. Fetch current headlines from as many outlets as possible. Keep the 20 to 30 strongest.
2. Break each one down:
   - **Trigger:** betrayal, secret, transformation, revenge, shock reveal, "I was wrong", money, health scare, family drama, humiliation, vindication.
   - **Specificity device:** ages, amounts, time spans, place names, named objects.
   - **Tension device:** withheld detail, contradiction, confession, "then" twist, insider quote.
3. Rewrite each as a fill-in-the-blank pattern.
4. Map the offer onto the patterns. Keep the emotional shape, swap in the product's world. It should read like it came from a real-life page, not an ad.
5. Present each adapted opening with the source headline or pattern beside it.
6. For story-led formats (drama ads, advertorials, long emails, VSLs), steal the arc too: setup, crisis or humiliation, discovery, turning point, outcome, reflection. Write the piece as that arc.

### Fallback tabloid patterns (web unavailable)

- "I [did something shocking] and [unexpected consequence]"
- "My [relative] [betrayed / shocked] me with [specific detail], then [twist]"
- "[Age]-year-old [ordinary person] reveals [secret] that [result]"
- "I spent [amount] on [thing] and [regret or triumph]"
- "Doctors told me [bad news], then [discovery]"
- "I was [humiliated] at [event], so I [response]"
- "Everyone said [common belief]. I found out the truth."
- "[Person] thought they were [state]. One [small thing] changed everything."
- "The [item] in my [place] that [consequence]"
- "I left [thing] for [other thing] and I have never looked back"
- "[Expert or insider] admits what [industry] does not want you to know"
- "She [did ordinary thing] for [time span]. Then [shock]."

## 5. Format rules

Apply after generating, before presenting.

**Email subject lines.** Under 50 characters where possible. Lowercase is allowed in the Settle lane. No emojis unless the brand uses them. Preview text continues the tension and never restates the subject.

**Email first lines.** One or two short sentences. No "Hope you're well". No restating the subject.

**Landing page and sales page headlines.** Lead with the outcome or the provocation, not the product name. Subheadline makes it concrete: who, what, how fast, with what proof. Keep headline under about 12 words.

**Static ad hooks.** First line of primary text is the hook; it must work before the "see more" cut (about 125 characters). On-image headline is 3 to 8 words.

**Video and UGC hooks.** Spoken line under 3 seconds (roughly 8 to 12 words). On-screen text echoes the tension, not the words. Pattern interrupt in the visual counts as part of the hook.

**Drama story ad openings.** First line drops the reader mid-crisis or mid-confession. Name a specific detail in the first sentence. No preamble.

**VSL hooks.** First 10 seconds: a claim or a scene, then a reason to stay ("in the next few minutes..."). Never open with "Hi, my name is".

**Blog, LinkedIn and Substack leads.** First line stands alone above the fold. Make it a hook from the Settle or tabloid lane, never a summary of the post.

## 6. Output format

Present openings grouped by lane, each labelled:

```
Settle lane
1. [opening]  (Settle: pattern name)

Tabloid lane
2. [opening]  (Tabloid: source outlet, original headline or pattern)

Direct lane
3. [opening]  (Direct: promise / proof / curiosity)
```

Then one line recommending which two or three to test first and why.
